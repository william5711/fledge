# Everyday how-to guide

Read this when a student asks how to handle a household task, repair, or landlord issue. Use it as a starting point, and add detail from your own knowledge when the question goes further.

## How to answer

1. Check the safety list first. If something matches, lead with that.
2. Give numbered steps they can follow right now, using only tools they probably own.
3. Say when to stop and call someone. In a rental, the landlord or property manager usually handles repairs, and an unauthorized repair can cost them money. In a dorm, it's the RA or housing maintenance.
4. Offer to save a follow-up as a reminder, or to add needed supplies to the grocery list.

Keep the tone matter-of-fact, because nobody is born knowing how to reset a breaker.

## Safety first

| What they notice | What to tell them |
| --- | --- |
| Gas or rotten-egg smell | Leave right away. Don't touch light switches, appliances, or flames. Once outside, call the gas company's emergency line or 911. |
| Carbon monoxide alarm | Get outside and call 911, even if nobody feels sick. Don't go back in until responders say it's safe. |
| Fire or smoke | Get out, close doors behind you, and call 911. Only use an extinguisher on a tiny fire with a clear way out. |
| Sparks, a burning smell from an outlet, or a hot panel | Stop using it. Shut off the breaker only if that's safe to reach, then call the landlord. Call 911 if there is smoke or flames. |
| Water near outlets or a sagging, wet ceiling | Stay away from the area and any standing water, and contact the landlord or maintenance immediately. Call 911 if there is danger right now. |
| Someone feels unsafe at home or is being threatened | Tell them to get somewhere safe and call 911 if the danger is immediate. In the US, campus safety and the 988 lifeline are also options for a crisis. |

Never suggest mixing cleaning products. Bleach with ammonia or with acids such as vinegar can release toxic gas.

## Laundry

1. Check clothing labels and sort out anything marked hand wash or dry clean.
2. Wash dark or new, brightly colored clothes apart from whites and light colors.
3. Use cold water for most loads. Use a warmer setting for towels and sheets if the labels allow it.
4. Follow the detergent's measuring line. More soap doesn't get clothes cleaner.
5. Clean the dryer's lint filter before each load. Lint buildup is a fire risk.
6. Air-dry anything that can shrink or that still has a stain, since dryer heat can set stains.

## Clogged sink or shower

1. Remove any visible hair or debris from the drain.
2. Use a plunger. Cover the overflow hole with a wet rag if there is one, then plunge firmly for about 20 seconds.
3. Try a cheap plastic drain snake for hair clogs.
4. If it's still clogged, contact the landlord or maintenance.

If they've already poured in chemical drain cleaner, they shouldn't plunge afterward because it can splash onto skin and into eyes. Tell them to tell the plumber what was used.

## Clogged or overflowing toilet

1. If the water is rising, turn the shutoff valve behind the toilet clockwise. They can also lift the tank's float to stop the water.
2. Don't flush again.
3. Use a flange plunger with slow, firm pushes.
4. Call maintenance if it won't clear or water reaches the floor.

## Power went out in one room

1. Unplug what was running when it stopped.
2. Find the breaker panel and look for a switch out of line with the others.
3. Push it fully to off, then back on.
4. If it trips again, stop and contact the landlord. For a bathroom or kitchen outlet, try the reset button on the outlet first.

## Smoke alarm that keeps chirping

A chirp every minute or so usually means the battery is low or the alarm is old. Replace the battery, or tell the landlord if it's hardwired or won't stop. Don't leave it disconnected.

## Food safety

- Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, or within 1 hour if it's above 90°F.
- Keep the fridge at or below 40°F and the freezer at 0°F.
- Eat refrigerated leftovers within 3 to 4 days.
- Thaw food in the fridge, in cold water, or in the microwave rather than on the counter.
- Store raw meat on the bottom shelf and use a separate cutting board for it.
- A "best by" date is usually about quality, not safety. When in doubt about spoiled food, throw it out. Tasting isn't a safe test.

## Writing to a landlord

Help them send a written message so there's a dated record:

> Hi [name], I'm in unit [number]. On [date] I noticed [problem and where]. [Effect, such as "the kitchen sink is draining very slowly".] I've attached a photo. Could you let me know when someone can look at it? I'm available [times]. Thanks, [name]

Suggest a follow-up reminder if there's no reply after a few days. Treat no heat, no water, or a safety problem as urgent, and call as well as writing.

## Moving in and out

- **Move-in photos:** take dated photos of every room, including existing damage, and send them to the landlord. These can help protect the security deposit, which is money held against damage or unpaid rent.
- **Move-out:** check the notice period in the lease, clean thoroughly, take photos, return keys, and give a forwarding address for the deposit.

## Legal questions

Tenant rules vary by location, so explain the general idea and don't claim what the law requires where they live. Suggest their lease, their school's off-campus housing office or student legal services, and a local tenant rights organization. For an eviction notice, tell them to get help right away because the deadlines can be short.
